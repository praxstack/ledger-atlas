# Ledger Atlas v1.0.0-test.6 (test build)

The sixth test build of the Mac app. It is signed ad hoc and not notarized, so macOS asks you to
approve it once: see the
[install guide](https://github.com/praxstack/ledger-atlas/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md). If an
earlier test build is installed, drag this one over it; your ledger and settings stay.

## New since test.5

- **Month and Year in Review.** Insights has a Statistics / Review switch in the toolbar.
  Review recaps any finished month or year: money in, spending and net compared with the period
  before, top spending categories, the biggest expenses, and activity (busiest day, days with no
  entries, budgets kept). A year also shows spending by month and its best and hardest months
  (#242).
- **To Review.** Entries the app adds for you wait in a new **To Review** list in the sidebar,
  with a count, until you've checked them: dates from a recurring rule, installment payments,
  rows from a spreadsheet import, and entries merged in from a backup. Entries you type never
  go there, and neither does your first Money Manager import (#241).
- **Selection total.** With several entries selected in Transactions, the bar shows what they add
  up to: spending, money in, or in, out and net (#240).
- **Search shows split purchases whole.** A search that matches part of a split purchase shows
  one row with the purchase total. A transfer between currencies shows the amount received and
  the rate (#235).

## Known limits

- The widget and Shortcuts are off in ad-hoc builds (they need an Apple Team ID).
- Backups made by the app are plain SQLite files; keep them somewhere private.
- Portable packages and the reopen at launch don't offer Choose Recovery Key… yet (#225). Use
  File › Open Ledger… to get it.
- Ledgers imported with an earlier test build keep the old debit-card balances; re-import to
  fix them (#223).
- Bulk edit can't delete entries yet.
- The first import of a phone-sized backup (about 7,500 entries) takes about 2 to 3 minutes.

## Checked before release

The shipped DMG ran on a CI Mac: it opened, walked every screen, imported a 7,500-entry made-up
backup and reopened the resulting ledger without errors. The release was published only after
the full CI run on the same commit passed.
